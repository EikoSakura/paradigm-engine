# Roadmap

1. **Scaffold** — done. system.json, folder layout (module, templates, styles, lang, fonts), en.json, entry script registering the character actor type and the trait and technique item types with empty TypeDataModels. Installs and loads clean in a new V14 world.
2. **Data models** — done. Character actor plus the two item types, fields from the spec comment blocks.
3. **Item sheets** — done. View and edit modes from the item sheets mockup.
4. **Character sheet, view mode** — done. Layout and styling from the mockup. The Traits and Techniques panels render the embedded items. Fonts bundled locally (Shippori Mincho B1, Zen Kaku Gothic New, both OFL).
5. **Character sheet, edit mode** — done. Title bar toggle, all fields editable. Add creates an embedded item, a card opens its item sheet, delete removes it. Pips, Luck spent, Advancement boxes, and the unspent counter stay clickable in both modes.
6. **Adversary actor type** — done. Desire, Fear, Rank, the five Attributes, Hearts, Energy, embedded Traits and Techniques. Rivals add Luck Dice. Reuse sheet parts.
7. **Packaging** — done. Release zip, manifest and download URLs, install test from the manifest.
8. **Chat cards** — done. Item and roll cards from the chat cards mockup. Post buttons on the item sheet title bar and on each sheet card. Rolls start from an Attribute or Technique name in view mode; the Action Roll dialog picks the second die and a Trait, backed by a real Roll. No Difficulty, no outcome text.
9. **Party folder** — done. A Party folder in the Actors directory, created once per world. The Party HUD shows only the characters inside it (subfolders count), in directory order, instead of every player-owned character. The folder can be renamed or moved; membership follows the folder.
10. **Distance** — done. Token distance measured edge to edge and counted in tiles: one tile is the scene's distance setting, gridless scenes included, and within one tile the pair is Engaged. `game.system.api.getDistance(tokenA, tokenB)` returns units, tiles, engaged, and label. While a token drags, the ruler label counts the tiles of displacement from the drag origin. With one token controlled, hovering another visible token draws a tinted range line labeled with the distance. Display only. Replaces the earlier walls-and-regions feature, which a migration strips from old worlds.
11. **Rules revision** — done. Grade renamed to Rank everywhere; the Ch2 Special is Rank Up Rank, and a migration moves stored grades. Roll bands renamed Critical Success, Success, Failure; Fumble unchanged. Openings removed — Sync replaces them at the table, with no system piece. The named Distance steps retired for tile counts; Engaged survives and means adjacent tile. The Ladder gains Die Value (E 4, D 6, C 8, B 10, A 12, S 14) for Technique range, Technique area maximum, and Sync range through Bonds.
12. **Fumble, grid, Positioning** — done. The Luck rider reads "cannot alter". Every scene measures 1 tile per grid space, units "tiles": the system default for new scenes, a preCreate and preUpdate rewrite for created and imported maps (most arrive in feet), and a migration for existing ones. The grid is cardinal — square grids count diagonals as 2 (Rectilinear, the default and set by the migration) and the system's own measurement adds the axis gaps the same way. The per-die Rank Up and Rank Down steppers already in the roll dialog carry Flanking and High Ground. Move (base 5) and the creation trade: the sheet vitals gain a Move plate with an edit stepper, character Hearts, Energy, and Move steppers clamp 3 to 8, adversaries take a free-range Move of their own, and the creator's Finishing Touches trade the three one for one — floor 3, cap 8, total 17 — with a Free counter that gates Create Character.
13. **Bond Ranks, Move advancement** — done. Bonds are a name and a Rank, E to S — the number and the sentence both go. A character starts with one Bond at E and holds at most six; Invoke at the table rolls the Bond's Rank die to replace one Luck Die, or activates a Sync, with no system piece. No growth channel yet: the Rank stays hand-editable, no automation. A migration maps stored numbers 1 to 6 onto E to S (6 is S) and deletes the number and sentence fields. The Standard Advancements list gains +1 Move, one box, under New Energy; Move stays capped at 8.
14. **Energy** — no build needed. A Technique's Energy cost equals its Rank number, 1 for E through 6 for S — the Ladder's mod column already prices it, so sheets, creator, and cards read right. The rest is table rules with no system piece: a Combo replenishes 1 Energy, Charge spends the action to recover 2, Scene end recovers 1, and a completed Relaxation Scene or Episode end restores all.
15. **Adversaries** — done. The stat line is Desire, Fear, Rank — public, the Difficulty to act against it — the five Attributes, Hearts, Energy, Move, Traits, and Techniques; pools and Move are Storyteller-set with the character line of 6, 6, and 5 as reference, so adversaries gain Move alongside the character Move build. Adversary results carry two rows only: at the Difficulty and above it gets what it is aiming for, below it does not, and a Fumble fails — so adversary roll cards drop the Critical band and every rider, and Opening creation is already gone. Getting what it is aiming for costs the target 1 Heart, and adversaries can Limit Break — rules text. Rivals keep three Luck Dice by character rules and re-rank on return, both already in place.
16. **Contests, Limit Break** — no build needed. Limit Break mechanics are unchanged and the band names already read Critical Success and Success.
17. **Areas** — done. An area Technique covers tiles within its reach, no wider than its Die Value from the point of origin; everything covered is a target unless the description says otherwise. One roll for the whole area, compared to each Difficulty within; the Spotlight reads the highest Difficulty among the targets — table rules, no automation. The build: an Area checkbox on Techniques and Traits in the item header's chip row, and an Area chip on the item sheet, sheet cards, and chat cards; clicking it opens the Size dialog — 1 to the item's Die Value, default the Die Value, counted in Tiles — and Place previews the burst on the canvas: a click drops it, Escape or a right click cancels. V14 keeps Measured Templates as previews only, so the dropped burst persists as a polygon Drawing in the user's color — the cardinal staircase diamond, corner-anchored on even widths and center-anchored on odd — moved and cleared with the core drawing tools. Below the Trusted drawing permission the shape flashes for a moment instead of persisting. When the rolled Technique is an area one — first or second die — the Roll Dialog carries the same Size stepper and Place button, so one window covers the whole activation; the chip stays for Traits and for placing without a roll.
18. **Ranks panel, description commit** — done. The sheet's Ladder panel is titled Ranks and its header row sits on an ink band. The S die is d12+2, so its Die Value 14 reads straight off the die; SS stays Difficulty 22. Item descriptions edited from a character's sheet commit reliably: leaving Edit or closing the window submits the open editor, and a mid-edit re-render — a stepper, a new Ruling — carries the unsaved text across instead of dropping it.
19. **Free vitals, creator resume** — done. Hearts, Energy, and Move on the sheets run free from 0 with no cap, characters and adversaries alike; the 3-to-8 trade totalling 17 lives in the Character Creator alone. The creator seeds from the actor — name, Attributes, first two Traits and Techniques, Bond, pools, Luck — and keeps every change on an actor flag, so reopening resumes the selections for editing instead of starting over. Create Character is safe to run again: grown Rank, extra Bonds, and Luck Die sizes survive, and only the creator's own Trait and Technique rows are replaced.
20. **Tension removed** — done. Tension is out of the game, so the meter, its settings menu, the Announce and Auto Gain options, the gain chips, and the Tension rider lines on roll cards are gone; Success carries no rider, Failure the Spotlight, Fumble the Spotlight and Luck. The Tracks board docks top center where the meter stood. A migration deletes the stored Tension settings and the applied marks on old roll cards. Its mockups are deleted.
21. **Tracks removed** — done. Tracks are out of the game, so the Tracks board, the New Track dialog, Contests, Escalations, the scene-control toggle, the fill and erase chips on roll cards, and the Adversary Builder's Desire Track option are gone. A migration deletes the stored Track list and the applied marks on old roll cards. Its mockups are deleted.
22. **Mockups removed** — done. Every mockup HTML file is deleted from the repo and the folder, the ignored ones included; the system code is the only reference.
23. **Traits removed, Techniques renamed Skills** — done. Traits are out of the game: the Item type, its sheet panel, the Ranks table's bonus column, the Trait pick and bonus on the Action Roll and its card, the creator and builder steps, the hotbar's Trait slots, and the New Trait and Rank Up Trait advancements are gone. Techniques are Skills everywhere — Item type `skill`, the Skills panel, the Skill bar and its keybindings, New Skill and Rank Up Skill, the lang keys and CSS. A migration deletes Trait Items, retypes Technique Items to Skills with their data intact, clears emptied hotbar slots, and moves the advancement boxes to the Skill keys; the character model carries the old box keys forward on load so nothing is lost before it runs. Legacy `-=` deletion keys in the migrations give way to the V14 ForcedDeletion operator.
24. **Party HUD and Adversary frame removed** — done. The character status cards over the left edge of the board and the adversary status cards against the sidebar are gone, with their templates, styles, and lang. The Party folder is no longer created or read by the system; an existing one stays as an ordinary Actors folder and a migration drops its flag. The Skill bar keeps the glass skin on its own.
25. **Move removed** — done. Play is theater of the mind, so Move is out of the game: the field on characters and adversaries, the Move plate and stepper in the sheet vitals, the creator's Move pool, and the +1 Move advancement are gone, with their lang and CSS. The creator trades Hearts and Energy one for one, totalling 12. A migration writes each actor's system back without the stored Move and clears it from unlinked token deltas and saved creator drafts.
26. **Difficulty revised** — done. The Difficulty numbers are E 5, D 7, C 9, B 11, A 13, S 16, SS 19, in the Ladder, the Difficulty picker, and the sheet's Ranks table.
27. **Distance and Areas removed** — done. Play has no maps, so both are out of the game: the tile ruler, range line, grid defaults and grid rewrites, and `game.system.api.getDistance` are gone, and so are the Area flag on Skills, the Area chips on sheets, item sheets, and chat cards, the Size dialog, burst placement, and the Roll Dialog's Area row, with their lang and CSS. A migration writes each Skill's system back without the stored Area flag; scenes keep whatever grid they have.
28. **Die Value removed** — done. The Ladder no longer carries a Die Value; nothing read it once range and area were gone. Each Rank is its letter, Die, Energy cost, and Difficulty.
29. **Character sheet revamp** — built, awaiting verification. The character sheet wears a white anime file skin in place of the ink-and-paper obi: a dark title bar, standing art on the left that overlaps the header and the Attributes card, the name in Reggae One over a blue rule with Player, Age, and Pronouns beneath, Hearts as pink heart icons and Energy as blue bolts (empty ones grey), the Rank as a large letter that flips to its Difficulty on click, and the three Luck Dice as blue hexes (spent ones grey). The tabs are small tags at the top right, and the title bar's View | Edit pair is a single Edit switch on every sheet. Attributes sit in one card as five columns of name and Rank letter, no descriptions, each letter flipping to its die; Skills and Bonds are cards with skewed colored tags, Skills as rows with the Rank chip, kind and Energy pills, and the post button; the Ranks table is gone from the character sheet. Growth and Profile keep their panels on the same cards. The skin is scoped to the character sheet; the adversary sheet keeps the old look until its own revamp. Mochiy Pop One (tags) and Reggae One (titles) are bundled as latin subsets under the OFL.
30. **Skill cost, Techniques and Talents** — built, awaiting verification. A Skill's Energy cost follows its Rank in pairs: E and D cost 1, C and B cost 2, A and S cost 3, read from the Ladder's cost column wherever it shows. The two kinds are Technique and Talent in place of Activated and Triggered: a Technique spends its cost when rolled, a Talent is always on, costs nothing, and shows no cost chip on the item sheet, sheet rows, chat card, Skill bar, Roll Dialog, Creator, or Builder. The Skill model reads the old kind names as the new ones, and the 0.4.0 Skill write-back stores them.
31. **Skill rows in two columns** — built, awaiting verification. The character sheet's Skills card lays its rows out in two columns, one on narrow sheets. Each row stacks: the name with the post and delete buttons on the first line, the kind and Energy pills on the second, the description below; a long name clips with an ellipsis. In Edit, a grip at the row's left edge drags a Skill onto another to reorder them, using the sheet's built-in Item sort. The sheet opens at 920 wide and the Bonds column narrows to 240 so both Skill columns have room; rows divide with a hairline between the columns and between lines, and the name sits flush left.
32. **Adversary sheet on the skin, Desire and Fear removed** — built, awaiting verification. The adversary sheet wears the same white anime file skin as the character sheet: the shared header with standing art, name over a crimson rule, Hearts and Energy pips, and the Rank letter that flips to its Difficulty; the eyebrow reads Adversary or Rival and in Edit becomes the Rival switch; Luck hexes show for Rivals only. No tabs: the body is the Attributes card and the Skills card at full width in two columns. Desire and Fear are out of the game — the fields, the sheet panel, the Builder inputs, and their lang — and the 0.4.0 actor write-back clears stored values. The header, Attributes card, and Skills card are shared partials; the ink-and-paper rail, vitals strip, Skills panel, adversary header, and their styles are deleted.
33. **PC and NPC, Rivals removed** — built, awaiting verification. The two actor types are PC and NPC everywhere: type ids `pc` and `npc`, the PC File and NPC File sheets, the PC Creator and NPC Builder, the data models, sheet classes, templates, lang, and CSS. Rivals are out of the game: the Rival flag, the NPC Luck Dice, the Builder's Finishing step, and the header switch are gone, so NPCs never hold Luck. A migration retypes every actor and pinned token delta, writes each NPC's system back without the Rival flag and Luck, and moves the NPC mark on stored roll cards to its new key.
34. **Skill File, Rulings removed** — built, awaiting verification. The Skill sheet wears the anime file skin: a square icon at the left that opens the file picker in Edit, the Skill eyebrow, the name in Reggae One over a pink rule, the kind and Energy pills beneath it — in Edit the kind is the Technique and Talent pair of buttons — and the Rank box at the right that flips to its die, with steppers in Edit. The body is one Description card holding the prose; in Edit it is the toggled core editor as PF2e uses it — a pencil opens the editor in place and Save closes it. Rulings are out of the game: the field, the sheet panel, the lines on sheet rows and chat cards, their lang and styles; the 0.4.0 Skill write-back clears stored lists. The old ink-and-paper item styles are deleted.
35. **Prerequisites and the Skill Tree** — built, awaiting verification. The Skill Tree is the Storyteller's to build, from the Skill Tree button in the Items directory: only Skills placed in it appear. A Skill dragged in from the Items sidebar joins as a root; dropped onto a node it becomes that node's specialization, and a node can be dragged onto another to move it; the mark on a node takes it out and its own specializations become roots. A chain cannot loop. A specialized Skill has no Rank of its own, so its sheet shows the foundation as a read-only row, its Rank box reads the foundation's Rank, and the steppers go. On an actor the chain resolves beside it: a Skill taken from the world carries the UUID it came from, the actor sheet's drop and the tree both stamp it, and the PC sheet shows the foundation's name as a pill and dims a Skill whose foundation is missing. From a PC sheet's tree button the window marks each node Known, Available when its foundation is Known, or Locked, and a take button on an Available node copies that Skill onto the PC. Clicking a node selects it and a strip along the bottom of the window shows that Skill's Rank, kind, cost, state, and description with an Open Sheet button; clicking it again clears the strip. An empty tree shows a dashed outline where the first root lands, and while a drag is over the window the node under it, or the whole tree for a root drop, outlines in blue. The NPC sheet's tree button opens the world tree.
36. **Action Roll popup** — built, awaiting verification. The Action Roll is a popup instead of a window, after the Anime was a Mistake system's roll menu: it hangs under the clicked Attribute or Skill name, or the Skill bar slot, with a tail, flips above when there is no room below, and closes on Escape or a click outside; Enter rolls. The header carries the first die's name, Energy pill, and stepper. The Second Die is two checkbox lists, the Attributes and then every Skill, Techniques and Talents alike, in alphabetical order with its die and Energy pill; every die is a dark tile, only the picked row shows its stepper, and a Technique the actor cannot pay for is dimmed and cannot be picked. The Difficulty is a row of letter-over-number tags, the picked one in its Rank color; Limit Break is a super meter: one skewed segment per step the Difficulty can still rise, filled through the clicked one in pink with the last in red, a Limit label beside it, and a flash when the gauge is full; the gauge takes its own line under the Limit Break label, with the shift at that label's end. The footer holds the formula with the target and the Roll button. Bonus is gone. The popup wears its own light skin on Alegreya Sans and Bangers, bundled under the OFL.
37. **Dark theme** — built, awaiting verification. A Theme setting, per user, in the system settings: Light, the default, Dark, or Match Foundry, which follows the Applications color scheme in Foundry's own settings. Dark inverts the anime file skin on the PC, NPC, and Skill Files, the Skill Tree, and the Action Roll popup: near-black window body, charcoal cards and title bar, light text, the same pink, blue, and Rank colors; empty Hearts, Energy, and Luck read as dark grey. The setting is a body class, so it switches live with no reload.
38. **Clashes** — built, awaiting verification. A Clash is an extended contest between two to eight sides, run from its own window: the swords button in the Token controls opens it, it opens on every client when a Clash begins, and Show opens it on every connected player's screen at any time. A side is one or more characters: an actor dragged in from the Actors directory on open ground forms a side, dropped on a side it joins that side, a folder dropped in arrives as one group, Add Selected adds each controlled token as its own side, and a side chip dragged onto another merges them; a chip's name edits in place and otherwise reads the first member with a count. Each side is an arm of boxes around a central diamond — two sides make a line, more fan out evenly — with its members' portraits fanned at the tip, three at most and a count past that. The Storyteller sets the target with a stepper, one to six, and presses Begin. A click on a box marks the arm through it, a click on a marked box unmarks from it; the first arm filled wins, the window names the winner, and every other side reads its Hearts loss, the winner's marks minus its own, applied at the table. The click that fills the arm posts one chat card with every side's boxes and result, and under a group each member with the same result. End Clash clears the sides and keeps the target. One Clash at a time, held in a world setting; players see the window read-only. The window wears the anime file skin and follows the Theme setting.
39. **Encounter Tracker removed** — built, awaiting verification. The glass Encounter Tracker is out: the Spotlight, raised hands, the participant combatant type, the Encounter templates, lang, and styles. Foundry's own combat tracker, initiative, turns, and rounds run encounters. The Clash opens from a swords button in the Token controls instead of the tracker header. A migration deletes stored encounters that still hold a participant combatant.
40. **Luck Dice edit** — built, awaiting verification. One Luck Die size per PC, d6 to d12, on the actor instead of each slot: the row label reads Luck Dice with the size beside it and, in Edit, the Rank-style steppers either side; raising or lowering clamps recorded values to the new die. In Edit each hex holds its recorded value as an input, so the three hexes keep their size and shape, with a small die button under each that rolls that die and records it unspent, and Roll All beside them for all three; rolls go to chat. In View a click on a hex marks it spent, as before. The per-slot size selects and the die label under each hex are gone. The PC Creator writes the three rolled values alone, so a grown size survives re-creation. The model carries the largest stored slot die up to the new field on load, and the 0.4.0 write-back stores it.
41. **Checks and the Attributes column** — built, awaiting verification. The PC and NPC Files put the Attributes in a list under the portrait, which now sits in a framed card at the top of the left column; the Skills card runs the middle, and a Checks card stands over Bonds on the right. Group Action: the leader targets the supporters, picks the Difficulty, and a shared card posts; each supporter rolls from their row against it, every Success earns the leader a Shift, and after the leader rolls a Shift raises one of their dice by one, up to its size. Opposed Action: the roller targets the other participants; everyone rolls from the shared card, the highest total wins, the rest read −1 Heart, a tie stands as a tie. Clash: Begin posts one live card that follows every mark, each member rolls from it with the Difficulty picked as usual, a Success marks one box on its side through the Storyteller's client, or from a Mark button later, and the card becomes the result when a side wins; Open Clash on the card opens the window, and the sheet's Clash Check rolls the same way for a member of a running Clash. The Action Roll popup picks the first die from the five Attributes when it opens from a Check, and an S die's +2 now counts in a card's total.
42. **Party File** — built, awaiting verification. A Party actor is a folder as much as a sheet: creating one creates an Actors folder named after it, holding it, and every PC inside that folder, or a folder under it, is a member. A PC dropped on the Party File moves into the folder; one dragged into the folder in the directory joins the same way; the cross on its card in Edit moves it out. The party and its folder keep each other's name, deleting the party leaves the folder and the PCs where they are, and the Party stands out in the directory with a party icon. The sheet is a menu of pages on the left, the party emblem above them: Members, Notes, and a Clash entry that opens the Clash window. The party name stands over the page. Members shows one card per PC, read live: the art with the Rank letter at its top right and the three Luck Dice at its foot, the name over the blue rule, the Player line, Hearts and Energy pips, and Open and Group buttons. Group Actions draw their supporters from the leader's Party, from a PC File's Group Action button or a card's Group button alike; a leader with no party still posts the card, and the Storyteller drops any character onto the posted card to add it as a supporter until the leader rolls. Opposed Actions keep targeting. Notes is a shared text page.
43. **Invoking Bonds** — built, awaiting verification. Each Bond row on the PC File carries an Invoke button in play; it opens a popover under the row with the three benefits, and once one is taken the row reads Invoked until the scene changes. Borrow Strength opens the Action Roll popup with the Bond's Rank die locked as the first die in an Attribute's place, tagged Bond, and the second die picked from the five Attributes and the Skills. Twist of Fate puts the Luck hexes into a pick state, dashed in yellow: clicking a spent hex replenishes it with a fresh roll, clicking a ready one rerolls it, and Done ends the pick and posts the three values. Sync spends 1 Energy and is off at 0. Every invocation posts a Bond card naming the character, Bond, and benefit. Invoked is a flag on the Bond row; in Edit the chip clears on click, and the Party File's menu gains New Scene, for the Storyteller, which clears every member's Bonds. The popover, chips, and pick state follow the Dark theme; the popover flips above its row when the tab would clip it below. The six-Bond cap is gone: the Bonds tag counts the rows and New Bond always shows in Edit.
44. **Growth Points** — built, awaiting verification. Advancement is Growth Points spent on the spot: the Growth tab's Milestone card awards an Episode 1 GP, an Arc 3, a Season 5, or, for the Storyteller, a Timeskip of any amount with the option tiers it reaches picked by toggles; the points show as a number and pips, and Cancel closes an award early, logging what was bought. Three cards hold the options, each 1 GP: General (Modify Skill, New Skill, Skill Rank Up I, New Bond, Bond Rank Up I) under any milestone, Arc (+1 Heart, +1 Energy, Step Up Luck Dice) under an Arc or Season, Season (Skill Rank Up II, Bond Rank Up II, Unlock Luck Die, Rank Up Character Rank, Rank Up Attribute) under a Season; Arc and Season options lock after one Take per milestone, a card outside the award is dimmed, and each option reads its live figure beside it — the Rank cap, the pool against 12, the next die, the Luck Dice against 5. Hearts, Energy, Luck size, Luck count, and Character Rank apply on Take; the rest open a pick window on the file skin: New Skill names and creates a Rank E Skill, Technique or Talent, lists the Skills the PC can take from the Skill Tree, and takes a Skill dropped in; New Bond names a Rank E Bond, lists the PC's Party and the NPCs, bonded ones dimmed with their Rank, and takes a character dropped in; Modify Skill lists the Skills and opens the picked one in Edit; the Rank Ups list the Skills, Bonds, or Attributes with the current and next Rank, those at the cap dimmed — Skills and Bonds cap at the Character Rank, Attributes at S. The last point closes the milestone into the Growth Log, newest first, and posts a card of what was bought. Unlock Luck Die adds a fourth and fifth hex, rolled on the spot; the Creator keeps the extra hexes through a re-create. The Unspent counter and the Standard and Special box grids are gone with their lang; the 0.4.0 actor write-back drops the stored boxes.
45. **Profile tab** — built, awaiting verification. The PC File's Profile tab wears the card skin like Main and Growth: an Identity card across the top with Player, Age, Gender, and Pronouns as four boxed cells, Personality and History side by side beneath it, History the wider, and Notes at full width, each card with its own tag. The three text fields hold formatted text: in View they render as prose, in Edit each is the core editor open in place, saved on leaving Edit or closing the File. Plain text written before becomes one paragraph per line on load, and the 0.4.0 actor write-back stores it. The Background panel is gone.
46. **Chat cards on the skin, Combo, Spotlight removed** — built, awaiting verification. Every chat card wears the white anime file skin: a white card with a soft shadow, a skewed tag off its top edge naming the kind — Action Roll, Technique or Talent, Bond, Growth, Group Action, Opposed Action, Clash — with the Clash side, tier, or Target as its tail, the title in Reggae One, the Rank as a colored square, dice as bordered squares with Reggae One numerals and lucky ones in gold, the total large at the right, and the result as a skewed colored band beside the Difficulty chip: Combo gold, Success green, Failure grey, Fumble pink. Shared cards and the Clash card are ruled rows with portraits; the Group Action's leader sits in an outlined box with the Shift arrows under the dice; the winner of an Opposed Action reads in pink and each loser its Heart. The bands are Fumble on double 1s, Combo on doubles of 6 or higher, read before the Difficulty, then Success or Failure against it; Critical Success is gone, PCs and NPCs read the same way, and a Combo counts as a Success for Shifts and Clash marks. The Bonus line is gone — an S die's +2 counts in the total silently. The Spotlight is out of the game: Failure carries no rider and Fumble only the Luck one. A Clash roll card ends with its side's name and boxes as they stood, and marks on its own through the Storyteller's client, when it posts or when a Luck swap makes the Success; the box it filled shows in pink and the Mark button is gone. The card is the whole message: Foundry's header line is hidden, and a dark band at the card's top carries the speaker's token art cut in on a slant at the left — the actor's portrait when the speaker has no token — the alias in Reggae One with PC or NPC under it, Foundry's own time and delete control, and the kind tag hanging off the band's foot. The cards follow the Theme setting: white in Light, charcoal with a deeper band in Dark, and Match Foundry follows Foundry.
47. **Portrait framing** — built, awaiting verification. A portrait is framed once on the actor and shown the same way everywhere: in Edit on the PC and NPC Files the picture drags inside its frame to set the focus, a − and ＋ pair under it zooms from 1 to 3, and a picture button beside them opens the file picker. The framing is a focus point and a zoom held on an actor flag; the File's portrait card, the Party File's member cards, and the chat band all cover their frame with that focus held still as the zoom changes. The chat band shows the actor's framed portrait, falling back to the token's art when the actor has none; Foundry's default icon and any other SVG count as none, so the band goes without art rather than stretching an icon. The Edit figure no longer opens the picker on click.

Foundry's own macro hotbar shows again and the 1 to 0 keys are its. A migration drops the stored slots from unlinked tokens; world actors lose theirs in the 0.4.0 write-back.
49. **Encounter tracker: free pick, raised hands** — built, awaiting verification. Foundry's combat tracker is picked freely on the file skin: no initiative, rows in the order combatants joined, each a portrait, the name in Reggae One, and PC or NPC under it. The Storyteller clicks a name to make that character the acting one, marked by a pink bar and Acting; Begin starts the encounter with no one acting, Previous and Next pass the turn along the list, End closes it, and a player whose turn it is can end it. 50. **PC Creator revamp, Dark default** — built, awaiting verification. The PC Creator is one scrolling file on the skin: the name in the header with a rail of the five steps that marks each done and scrolls to its card, then five tagged cards. Attributes spend five points from E up to A, with the points left on the card; Skills are two at Rank E, Technique or Talent, with the Energy chip on a Technique only; the Starting Bond takes a name and a Relation, Rank E, and a Party member or NPC dropped on the card; Luck Dice roll 3d6 into three hexes; Finishing Touches split eight points between Hearts and Energy from zero, with the Character Rank D stamp beside them. A checklist and Create PC close the file, Create gated until every card is complete. The old step wizard, its obi chrome, and the 3 to 8 trade totalling 12 are gone. Bonds carry a Relation, shown after the name on the PC File and edited there. The Theme setting defaults to Dark; Light is a choice.
51. **Starting Skills, Clash trades** — built, awaiting verification. A Starting Skills entry in the system settings opens a window on the skin where the Storyteller drops Skills from the Items Directory and removes them with a cross; the list is a world setting of Skill UUIDs. When it holds any, the PC Creator shows a Granted Skills card under the Skills card listing them with kind and Rank, free and outside the two picks, and Create PC copies them onto the PC at their Rank, tagged as granted so a re-create replaces them. Every Clash roll card carries a Trade button beside its boxes while the Clash runs: it opens a picker of the roller's own dice and every other roll of this Clash, any side, with theirs; picking one of each swaps the two values through the Storyteller, both cards re-band and show the traded die in gold with a line naming the trade, and the Clash marks follow — a roll that becomes a Success marks its box, one that stops being a Success gives it back. A Fumble on either card cannot trade, and the button goes once the Clash ends or no other roll is there. Clash roll cards now record which Clash they belong to.
52. **Skill Tree in the Creator, Choice Skills** — built, awaiting verification. The Creator's Skills card ends in a Skill Tree line with an Open Skill Tree button that opens the tree for this PC, and under it the tree's root Skills, each with Take: Take fills the next empty pick with that Skill's name, kind, and text, locks the pick and reads From the Skill Tree on it, and the row reads Taken, which clears the pick again. A Skill dropped on a pick from the Items Directory fills it the same way. Create PC copies a filled pick from its world Skill at Rank E, stamped with its source. The Starting Skills window holds two cards, Granted and Choice, each with its own drop list, and the Choice card's chip steps how many a PC picks, never more than the list holds. When the Choice list and count stand, the Creator shows a Choice Skills card between Skills and Granted Skills with a tick per Skill and a Left chip that locks the rest at the count; the checklist and Create PC wait for the picks, and Create copies them beside the granted ones, tagged so a re-create replaces them and reopening shows them ticked.
53. **NPC Builder on the skin** — built, awaiting verification. The NPC Builder is one scrolling file on the skin like the PC Creator: the name in the header with a rail of three steps that marks each done and scrolls to its card, then Attributes, Skills, and Hearts & Energy. Attributes spend five points from E up to A with the points left on the card; Skills are two at Rank E, Technique or Talent, with the Energy chip on a Technique only, the Skill Tree line with Open Skill Tree, the tree's root Skills with Take, and a Skill dropped on a pick filling it; Hearts and Energy step freely to twelve, with the Rank stamp beside them stepping E to S and flipping to its Difficulty on click. The builder seeds from the NPC, keeps its draft on the actor, and Create NPC replaces only the Skills it wrote before. A checklist and Create NPC close the file, Create gated until the name, the five points, and both Skills stand. The old step wizard and the last of the obi chrome are gone.

59. **Dossier on the Party File** — built, awaiting verification. A Dossier page between Members and Notes: a deck of NPC cards, five by seven. The GM drops an NPC on the Party File and it becomes a card reading the NPC's portrait, framing, and name live, with a standing stamp — Ally, Neutral, Enemy, Unknown — and a title on the front; a click flips the card to the back, where the GM's Affiliation, Last Seen, and Known stand under the name. In Edit the GM drags the portrait in its frame and zooms it as on the NPC File, writing the NPC's own framing; sets the standing and the title on the front, writes the back, flips with the rotate button, hides the card with the eye, and removes it with the cross. Players see a hidden card face down with a question mark; the GM sees its front with an eye-slash. The Dossier lives on the Party as system.dossier.
60. **Flip a die, Trade off Combos** — built, awaiting verification. Every roll card carries a Flip button in its foot, for anyone: pressed, the two dice turn pink and wait, and the one clicked turns to its other side, the die's size plus one minus the face it shows, so a 2 on a d6 becomes a 5 and a 1 on a d4 a 4; pressed again, the dice stand down. The flipped die reads blue with a line under the dice naming the flip and who made it, the total and the band read again, and on a Clash roll the box follows as it does for a trade. On Opposed and Group Action cards a flip glyph under each rolled die does the same for that row, beside the leader's Shift arrows. A card standing on a Combo or a Fumble offers no Flip, and Trade now follows the same rule: the button goes on a Combo as on a Fumble, and the picker greys the other rolls standing on either. The write goes through the Storyteller when the flipper may not edit the message.
61. **Opposed and Group Action setup window** — built, awaiting verification. Opposed Action and Group Action on the PC and NPC Files, and Group on the Party File, open a setup window on the skin instead of posting the card at once: a tagged card listing the starter first, Starter or Leader on its row, then the other participants with a cross to remove each, a dashed drop area, an Add Selected button, and Begin. Characters join by dragging a PC or NPC from the Actors Directory onto the window, a folder bringing every character in it, or by selecting tokens on the board and pressing Add Selected; a character already listed is not added twice. An Opposed Action seeds its rows from the user's targets and Begin waits for a second row; a Group Action seeds from the starter's Party and picks its Difficulty on a rank row in the window, Begin waiting for the pick. Begin posts the shared card as before, each row rolling from it. The Difficulty popup that Group Action opened, the targets warning, and the roll popup's Difficulty-only mode are gone.
62. **Copies of one character** — built, awaiting verification. The same PC or NPC may stand more than once in an Opposed Action, a Group Action, or a Clash, so one sheet serves a band of goblins. In the setup window a character dropped or added again takes another row, and in the Clash an Actor dragged in again or added from a second linked token joins as another member, on the side its first copy stands on, since a Clash roll finds its side by the character; the same token twice is still one member. Copies read their name with a letter after it, Goblin A, Goblin B, Goblin C, in the setup window, on the shared card's rows, in the Clash window's sides and tooltips, and on the Clash card's member rows; a character standing once keeps its plain name. The Roll button on a copy's Clash card row posts the roll under the copy's lettered name. All copies share the one sheet's Hearts and Energy.
63. **Limit Break outcomes** — built, awaiting verification. A Limit Break now pays out as the rules text says. On a Success in a Clash the roll marks one box plus one per Rank raised, and those boxes show as fresh on the card; a Luck swap, trade, or flip that unmakes the Success gives every box back. The card's Limit Break line names the outcome: in a Clash +N Boxes, elsewhere Target −N Hearts, and on a Failure or a Fumble Setback −N Hearts, the N there being 1 plus the Ranks raised. The Clash roll's flag keeps the boxes the roll holds.

66. **Combo must meet the Difficulty** — built, awaiting verification. A Combo only succeeds if the total meets the Difficulty: doubles of 6 or higher below the Difficulty band as a Failure, like any other roll, and no longer mark a Clash box, earn a Shift, or pay a Limit Break. The COMBO!! splash, and the Flip and Trade refusals, still read the dice as they stand.
67. **Combo recovers 1 Energy** — built, awaiting verification. A roll that bands as a Combo recovers 1 Energy for its roller, up to their maximum, once per card, with a Combo · +1 Energy line on the card; a Combo unmade by a Luck swap, trade, or flip gives it back. The card keeps the Energy it holds. The recovery runs where the card is written: on the roller's client when the roll posts, and on the Storyteller's for card ops, so a row op on a shared card now always relays through the Storyteller. The extra turn stays manual.
